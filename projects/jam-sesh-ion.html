Jam Sesh-ion!

A community web application designed to help musicians at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa discover collaborators and organize jam sessions.

  • Next.js
  • Prisma
  • UI/UX
  • Vercel
  • Team Project
jam-sesh-ion.reportREAD_ONLY

01. The idea

UH Mānoa has many students with musical talent, but finding people with compatible interests and schedules is not always simple. Jam Sesh-ion grew from that gap. Our team wanted to give students a focused place to share their musical identity, discover other musicians, and turn a possible connection into an actual session.

Users can build a profile around their tastes, skills, and goals, then add YouTube or SoundCloud links so other people can hear their work. Session tools help members create, join, and manage gatherings while clear availability indicators show whether a session still has space.

Technology mattered, but the purpose was human: make it easier for students to meet through music.

02. My contribution

I worked across the interface and data layers. On the front end, I designed and styled profile views, jam-session pages, and the session-creation flow. I focused on straightforward navigation and visual cues that would help a new user understand what to do next.

I also helped shape the Prisma schema, set up and migrate the database, and add sample data. For session management, I helped implement creation and joining behavior plus a color-coded availability system. User feedback led us to improve administrative controls and make profile interactions feel more complete.

03. Process and decisions

  • Start with a recognizable student need rather than a list of technologies.
  • Represent profiles and sessions clearly in the database before polishing the interface.
  • Use visual status cues so availability can be understood quickly.
  • Test assumptions with feedback and adjust the administrative experience.

04. What I learned

The project strengthened my front-end and back-end skills, but its biggest lesson was collaborative delivery. Dividing work, sharing progress, resolving overlaps, and listening to users affected the quality of the result as much as any individual feature.

I also learned to balance technical possibilities with the experience of the person using the application. Future directions could include group chat, richer scheduling, and expanded profile controls, but the first priority remains a clear path from discovery to connection.
