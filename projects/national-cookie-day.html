National Cookie Day

A student-led commercial bakery project about preparation, delegation, communication, and appreciation for school staff.

  • Leadership
  • Planning
  • Communication
  • Teamwork
national-cookie-day.reportREAD_ONLY

01. Context

During my senior year of high school, I led a Career and Technical Education project focused on operating a commercial bakery. National Cookie Day gave the project a practical theme: produce a large batch of cookies for faculty while using the work to show appreciation near the holiday season.

Before production, I researched bakery responsibilities, advertising, equipment, staffing, and workflow. During the project, I taught classmates how to use equipment, organized responsibilities, monitored production, and helped solve problems as they appeared.

02. When the plan met reality

Our first attempt exposed an important preparation gap: I had not converted the recipe ahead of time. The resulting confusion showed me that enthusiasm and effort are not enough when other people depend on your instructions.

I learned to slow down, prepare details before the work begins, assign clear roles, and communicate at the team’s pace. Those changes made the project more organized and gave everyone a better understanding of what they owned.

Leadership is not simply being in charge. It is creating enough clarity for other people to do their best work.

03. Growth

The project helped me understand the connection between preparation and confidence. It also made me more aware of my public-speaking habits, including relying too much on notes. Since then, I have continued working toward presenting more naturally, maintaining eye contact, and internalizing material before I speak.

04. What I carried forward

The most lasting lessons were adaptability, shared ownership, and appreciation. The final result depended on everyone’s contribution, and the mistakes were as useful as the successes. I still bring those lessons into group projects: prepare carefully, communicate clearly, and adjust when a real team meets a real deadline.
