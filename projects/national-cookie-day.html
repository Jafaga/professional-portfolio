National Cookie Day

A student-led commercial bakery project centered on preparation, delegation, mass production, and appreciation for school faculty and staff.

01 / OVERVIEW

The project at a glance

National Cookie Day gave the assignment a practical goal: organize a team, operate the bakery, and produce cookies as a gesture of appreciation near the holiday season.

THE ASSIGNMENT

Lead a commercial bakery project

Research the operation, plan the workflow, teach classmates, and oversee a real production process.

THE RESPONSIBILITY

Turn preparation into clear direction

I organized roles, explained equipment and steps, monitored progress, and helped solve problems as they appeared.

THE RESULT

A stronger understanding of leadership

The project showed me that a team performs better when the person leading has prepared the details and communicates them clearly.

02 / RESPONSIBILITIES

What I owned as project lead

RESEARCH

Bakery operations

I studied responsibilities, advertising, equipment, staffing, and workflow before production began.

INSTRUCTION

Equipment and process

I taught classmates how to use the bakery equipment and explained how each person’s task connected to the full production.

COORDINATION

Roles and timing

I delegated responsibilities, monitored the work, and adjusted the plan when the real process differed from my expectations.

DELIVERY

Purpose beyond the assignment

The cookies became a way to thank faculty and staff who supported us throughout school.

03 / CHALLENGE

When the first plan met reality

One missing preparation step became the most useful leadership lesson in the project.

WHAT HAPPENED

The recipe had not been converted

On our first attempt, I had not completed the conversion ahead of time. The uncertainty created confusion for everyone following my instructions.

WHAT CHANGED

I slowed down and clarified ownership

I learned to prepare details before production, assign specific roles, and communicate at the team’s pace.

WHY IT MATTERED

Clarity created confidence

When each person understood the plan and their responsibility, the group could work more independently and effectively.

04 / WORKFLOW

A repeatable production process

  1. 01PrepareResearch the operation, convert recipes, confirm tools, and define the production goal.
  2. 02AssignGive each person a clear responsibility and explain how the roles connect.
  3. 03ProduceMonitor quality and timing while helping the team solve real-time problems.
  4. 04ReflectIdentify what created confusion and improve the communication for the next attempt.
05 / TAKEAWAYS

What I carried into later team projects

LEADERSHIP

Being in charge is not the same as being clear

Leadership means giving other people enough preparation, context, and ownership to do their best work.

COMMUNICATION

Preparation changes how confidently I speak

The experience also pushed me to rely less on notes, maintain eye contact, and internalize material before presenting.

CORE LESSON

The final result depended on everyone’s contribution. The mistakes were as useful as the successes because they showed me exactly where planning and communication needed to improve.
