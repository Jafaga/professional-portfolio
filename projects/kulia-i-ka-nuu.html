Kūlia i Ka Nuʻu

“Strive for the highest”—a months-long research and presentation project about homelessness in the local community.

  • Research
  • Presentation
  • Teamwork
  • Community
kulia-i-ka-nuu.reportREAD_ONLY

01. The project

During my final year at Konawaena Middle School, every eighth-grade student selected a real-world issue to research and present. My group chose homelessness because it was visible in our community and because we wanted to understand both its causes and its effects more carefully.

Over several months, we reviewed local statistics, studied the resources available through shelters and aid programs, and examined how homelessness affects individuals and the wider community. The goal was not only to report information, but to create an engaging presentation that could encourage people to think about how they might help.

02. My role

I focused on researching why homelessness happens and what local resources are available. I also helped design the PowerPoint, organize the flow between speakers, and decide how much information each slide needed. We practiced repeatedly so the handoffs would feel natural and each person could speak with confidence.

I opened the final presentation in front of teachers, classmates, community members, and a review panel. Beginning the presentation was nerve-racking, but it pushed me outside my comfort zone and helped me become more comfortable speaking in front of an audience.

Kūlia i ka nuʻu means to strive for the highest. For me, the project meant taking an issue seriously enough to research it well and explain it responsibly.

03. What stayed with me

The experience strengthened my ability to find reliable information, turn research into a clear narrative, work through a long timeline with a team, and present professionally. Those are still skills I use in technical and nontechnical work today.

It also showed me that a project can be successful for more than its final presentation. The months of preparation, questions, revisions, and conversations were what made the work meaningful.
