What Community Service Taught Me About Showing Up

Reflections from Leo Club, Tagnawa, and the people who taught me that meaningful work begins with attention and service.

August 11, 20266 minute readCommunity
showing-up-for-community.logPERSONAL_NOTE

I grew up on Hawaiʻi Island surrounded by people who shaped the way I think about responsibility. Family, friends, teachers, club advisers, and community members taught me that giving back does not always begin with a large plan. Often, it begins with paying attention and being willing to show up.

Learning service through small, repeated actions

From 2019 through 2022, the Konawaena Leo Club gave me opportunities to participate in tree planting, night markets, and support for kūpuna. The activities were different, but each one required reliability. A service event depends on people arriving, sharing ordinary tasks, and staying engaged after the exciting part is over.

Those experiences changed how I understood leadership. It was less about being the most visible person and more about helping a group move together.

Community work after the Lahaina fires

In 2023, I supported Tagnawa’s Hawak Kamay outreach and call-center work for Filipino community members affected by the Lahaina fires and the October reopening. That work required empathy and careful listening. A person calling for information was not an abstract case; they were living through uncertainty and loss.

I learned that useful help starts by respecting what another person is actually asking for. Technology and organization can support that response, but they cannot replace patience or human care.

The lesson I keep returning to is simple: service is not about proving that I can help. It is about understanding what help is needed.

How this connects to the work I want to make

My background in Computer Science and Philippine Language and Culture gives me two ways to think about the same goal. I want to understand systems, but I also want to understand the people inside those systems—their language, history, needs, and community.

That is why I am interested in people-centered technology and in how AI can be applied to real situations responsibly. A technically impressive solution is not enough if it ignores the people it is supposed to serve.

A second life, not a separate life

Community, culture, family, and friendships are not distractions from becoming a computer scientist. They are part of the reason I care what technology does. They remind me that growth should be measured not only by what I can build, but by whether I become more useful, thoughtful, and present for other people.

I am still learning what that looks like. For now, I know it begins by showing up.
